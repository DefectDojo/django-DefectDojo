          Remediation Center (Pro)

          On this page

          One outdated library on one Asset can produce dozens of Findings, and every one of them is closed by the same upgrade. The Remediation Center turns open Findings into the work that fixes them. It groups Findings by the change that resolves them into remediation actions, one per change per location, and gives each action a due date, an owner, and a workflow status.

          The Remediation Center adds a layer on top of your Findings. It does not replace them. Findings stay the evidence underneath each action, and each Finding keeps its own status, SLA, and history. You keep triaging and closing Findings as before. The action tracks the result.

          Enabling the Remediation Center#

          The Remediation Center is a beta feature behind the remediation_center feature flag. A superuser can turn it on from Settings > Feature Flags. See Feature Flags.

          While the flag is off, the Remediation Center pages, its settings page and its API endpoints are unavailable.

          Actions are built from open Findings after each import, and once a night for every Asset. If the Action Queue is empty right after you turn the flag on, use Build Actions Now on the Overview page to group existing open Findings immediately.

          Where it appears#

          When the flag is on, a Remediation Center entry (marked BETA) appears in the MANAGE section of the sidebar, with three pages:

          • Overview: open Findings grouped into the changes that fix them, and how late that work runs.
          • Action Queue: the list of remediation actions, filterable by All Work, Assigned to Me and My Teams.
          • Campaigns: the same change rolled up across every Asset that needs it.

          Superusers also get Settings > Configuration > Remediation Center, where the SLA windows and due-date alerts are set (see Settings).

          What a remediation action is#

          A remediation action is one change on one place that resolves a set of Findings. “Upgrade example-lib from 1.2.0 to 1.4.1 in package-lock.json” is one action. The same upgrade on a different Asset is a different action.

          Each action records:

          • What to change. An action type, such as Upgrade, Patch, Configuration change, Code change, Rebuild, Removal, Certificate replacement, Permission change, Decommission, or Other. For component upgrades, the action also shows the current and target versions.
          • Where to change it. The Asset, plus a location within it: a host, a code location, a manifest, a container image, a web site, a cloud resource, or the Asset as a whole. An action never spans more than one Asset.
          • What it resolves. The Findings grouped under it, with counts of open Findings, CVEs and KEV entries, and the highest severity and risk among the open Findings.

          The target version is the highest fixed version the Findings name, so one upgrade covers every lower fix. Each action also carries a Grouping Rationale that shows why its Findings were grouped together, marked as an Exact Match, a Heuristic Match, or Grouped by Hand.

          A Finding belongs to at most one action. Closed Findings stay attached to their action, so an action keeps a record of everything it resolved.

          Adjusting the grouping#

          The grouping is a suggestion you can change. From an action you can:

          • Split Into a New Action: move some of its Findings into a new action.
          • Move to Another Action: move Findings into another open action on the same Asset.
          • Merge Other Actions Into This One.
          • Edit Action: change the title, action type, target version, or remediation steps.
          • Suppress: hide the action from work queues, with a reason. Restore to the Queue brings it back with its owner, due date and history as they were.
          • Hand Back to Automatic Grouping: return Findings you placed by hand to wherever automatic grouping puts them.

          Findings you place by hand stay where you put them when the Asset is regrouped. Fields you edit are left alone by later regrouping until you reset them.

          Due dates and SLA state#

          An action’s due date is the earliest SLA expiration date among its open Findings. The action records which Finding sets that deadline. The due date moves on its own when Findings are added, closed, reopened, or risk accepted.

          The action does not change the Findings’ SLAs. Each Finding keeps the SLA it was given by its Asset’s SLA configuration.

          Each action shows an SLA state computed from its due date:

          SLA stateMeaning
          OverdueThe due date has passed.
          At RiskDue within the At Risk Within (Days) window (3 days by default).
          Due SoonDue within the Due Soon Within (Days) window (14 days by default).
          On TrackDue later than both windows.
          No Due DateNone of the open Findings has an SLA date.

          Owners#

          Each action has two kinds of owner:

          • Assignee: the person carrying out the change. Assign from the action, or select several actions in the Action Queue and assign them at once. The assignee must be able to view the action’s Asset.
          • Team: the owning team. By default this is the group that owns the most open Findings in the action (the Finding’s owners group). You can change it from the action’s Ownership panel.

          Workflow status#

          An action moves through these statuses:

          StatusNotes
          NewThe starting status for an action.
          Planned
          In Progress
          BlockedRequires a reason saying what is blocking the action.
          Change ScheduledRequires the date the change is scheduled for. The date cannot be in the past.
          Awaiting ValidationUse this once the change is made and you are waiting for a rescan or review to confirm it.
          Exception Requested
          CompleteSet automatically. You cannot choose it.

          An action becomes Complete on its own when every Finding it resolves is closed. If at least one of those Findings was fixed (mitigated), the timeline records the action as validated. Closing every Finding as risk accepted, false positive, out of scope or duplicate also completes the action, without recording a fix. If a closed Finding reopens, or a new open Finding joins a complete action, the action reopens.

          Each action has an Activity timeline showing comments and every change: who made it and when.

          Tickets#

          From an action, Create Ticket gives you a ready-made description to copy into your tracker. You then link the ticket back to the action. The pattern is one ticket per action.

          Campaigns#

          A campaign groups every action that makes the same change, across all Assets. For example, upgrading example-lib to 1.4.1 on twelve Assets is twelve actions and one campaign. Campaigns are what a team lead plans from.

          Each campaign shows a rollup of its actions:

          • the number of actions, open actions and completed actions;
          • the number of Assets involved;
          • open Findings across all its actions;
          • the earliest due date among its open actions, and how many of them are overdue;
          • the highest risk among its actions;
          • progress, as the share of its actions that are complete.

          Campaigns are built and updated automatically. You do not create them by hand.

          Settings#

          Settings > Configuration > Remediation Center holds the instance-wide settings. Anyone with access can read them. Only superusers can change them.

          • SLA Windows: At Risk Within (Days) (default 3) and Due Soon Within (Days) (default 14) set the SLA state thresholds above.
          • Due-Date Alerts: Send Due-Date Alerts turns alerts on or off (on by default). Alert Days Before Due Date sets when they go out (30, 14, 7, 3 and 1 days before the due date by default).

          Alerts go to the action’s assignee, or to the owning team’s members while nobody is assigned. The Asset’s managers and contacts receive an escalation when an action is overdue, blocked close to its deadline, or coming due with no assignee and no owning team. Each person gets one message per day listing their actions, and only people who can view the action’s Asset are notified.

          Permissions#

          The Remediation Center follows the same Asset permissions as the rest of DefectDojo:

          • You see actions only for Assets you can view, and only the Findings you are allowed to see.
          • Changing status, assigning, setting the team, commenting and linking tickets requires permission to edit Findings on the Asset.
          • Changing the grouping (edit, split, move, merge, suppress, regroup) requires permission to edit the Asset.

          API#

          The Remediation Center is available on the public API for automation such as ticket bridges, reporting, and bulk assignment. Requests use a normal API token. The endpoints return an error while the feature flag is off.

          • /api/v2/remediation_actions/: list and retrieve actions, plus actions to change status, assign, set the team, comment, link a ticket, edit, split, move, merge, suppress, and export (CSV, Excel, or JSON).
          • /api/v2/remediation_campaigns/: list and retrieve campaigns with their rollups.
          • /api/v2/remediation_center_settings/: read the settings. Superusers can update them.

          See the API documentation on your instance for the full list of endpoints and fields.
