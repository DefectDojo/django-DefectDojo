This page takes a DefectDojo Pro instance from its first login to being ready for the EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA): the settings to turn on once, what to record on each product in scope, what to load for each release, how to handle an Article 14 report, and how to produce evidence each period. Each step says where to click, starting from the left menu, and what you should see when it worked.

The features it configures are described in full on their own pages: Regulatory Profile, CRA Article 14 Reporting and CRA and DORA Evidence Packs.

Everything here works with a standard DefectDojo Pro licence. None of it needs the PSIRT Advisory Engine.

DefectDojo records facts, tracks deadlines and assembles evidence. It does not decide whether the CRA applies to a product, which class the product is in, or whether a requirement is met. Those are your organization’s determinations; confirm them with qualified legal and compliance reviewers.

The examples use one product throughout: an IoT Gateway Asset whose release 4.1 ships log4j-core 2.13.2, and so carries Log4Shell (CVE-2021-44228). Use your own products and values.

Part 1 — Configure the instance#

Do these once, as a superuser.

Step 1 — Turn on the features#

In the left menu, go to Settings > Feature Flags. Type each feature’s name in Search Feature Flags and switch it on in the Enabled column:

Feature (as shown)What it gives you
Regulatory ProfileThe Regulatory card on each Asset, the CRA Article 14 Reports page, and the Article 14 deadline checks and notifications
ComplianceThe Compliance tab on each Asset. It may already be on
EU Evidence PacksCRA assessments and evidence packs. Needs Compliance
Dashboards 2.0The Regulatory Scope and CRA Article 14 Deadlines dashboard widgets
LocationsThe component inventory that SBOM imports write. It may already be on. It cannot be switched off once on, and asks you to confirm

No restart is needed. Leave every other flag as it is; this guide does not use them. See Feature Flags.

ExpectedWhere
CRA Article 14 Reports under ActThe left menu
A Regulatory card near the top of every Asset pageAny Asset
An EU Evidence tabAsset > Asset Overview > Compliance tab > Compliance Profile

Step 2 — Enable per-release SBOMs#

This one is a deployment setting, not a feature flag. On DefectDojo Cloud, ask DefectDojo Support to set DD_V3_ASSET_VERSIONS=True on your instance. On a self-hosted install, your administrator sets the environment variable DD_V3_ASSET_VERSIONS=True and restarts DefectDojo.

It lets each SBOM import name the release it describes, so you can show later which release carried which component. Without it, every SBOM lands on the Asset as a whole.

ExpectedWhere
A Version field in the Import SBOM dialogStep 10

Step 3 — Turn on known-exploited and EPSS enrichment#

The CRA’s Annex I Part I requires products to be made available without known exploitable vulnerabilities. The assessment’s PI-2(a) requirement, and the Article 14 prompt in Part 4, read that from the Known Exploited flag on findings, which enrichment sets from the CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog. EPSS scores come from the same enrichment.

  • DefectDojo Cloud: enrichment is on already. There is nothing to set.
  • Self-hosted: in the left menu, go to Settings > Finding Workflow > Finding Enrichment, tick KEV Lookup Enabled and EPSS Lookup Enabled, and select Submit. If the menu item is missing, ask DefectDojo Support whether your licence is marked as self-hosted.

Enrichment runs when findings are imported and once a night. PI-2(a) can read Satisfied only if an enrichment run that included KEV completed within 7 days of the assessment’s evidence date (the period end, or today while the period is still running).

ExpectedWhere
After the next import or nightly run, findings for CVEs in the CISA KEV catalog are marked Known ExploitedThe finding’s details, or the Known Exploited column on a findings list

Step 4 — Create your remediation policy#

In the left menu, go to Settings > Finding Workflow > Service Level Agreements, and select New Service Level Agreement.

  1. Service Level Agreement Name: name it for its purpose, for example CRA remediation.
  2. Description: optional; say which products it is for.
  3. Set Restart SLA on Finding Reactivation as your policy requires: ticked, a mitigated finding that a reimport reactivates gets a new deadline; unticked, it keeps its original one.
  4. Leave Service Level Configuration Type on Severity and SLA Start Policy on Detection Date (Default).
  5. Type the days you allow in Critical, High, Medium and Low Severity Finding Days (for example 7, 30, 90 and 180), and leave the four Enforce … Finding Days boxes ticked. The CRA requires remediation “without delay” but sets no numbers; use your own policy.
  6. Leave the FedRAMP VDR & PAIN Tiering boxes unticked, and select Submit.

Type every day count, including ones that look filled in. The boxes show 7, 30, 90 and 120 as grey placeholders, not values, and the form will not save until each has a number.

Create a new configuration rather than editing a shipped one such as Default. A shipped configuration is shared by every Asset that uses it, and changing it re-dates their findings too.

ExpectedWhere
CRA remediation is listed with your day countsSettings > Finding Workflow > Service Level Agreements

You assign it to products in Step 8.

Step 5 — Put CRA on a dashboard#

In the left menu, go to Overview > Dashboards.

  1. Select New Layout (the + beside the layout name), type a Layout Name such as EU CRA, and select Submit. A separate layout keeps the CRA widgets off your default dashboard.
  2. The new layout is empty. Select the Add Widget button in the middle of the page. On the By Type tab, select CRA Article 14 Deadlines. Adding a widget turns on edit mode.
  3. Select Add Widget again, now the orange + at the right of the toolbar, and select Regulatory Scope.
  4. To show only the CRA on Regulatory Scope, select the widget’s gear (Edit Widget), type CRA in Regulations, and save. The gear shows only in edit mode, which adding a widget turns on; otherwise select Edit Layout (the grid icon at the right of the toolbar) first.
  5. Select Done Editing (the check mark) to leave edit mode.

Regulatory Scope lists every regulation on your Assets, one row each: the Assets in scope, their open findings by severity, findings past their deadline, and Assets whose support period has ended or ends within 90 days. In 3.4.201 and 3.4.202 the per-severity columns undercount (they can read 1 where there are several findings); rely on the Assets, Past SLA and Support columns, and on each Asset’s own finding counts. It counts an Asset under the CRA from the Asset’s Regulations (Step 7), not from its Regulatory card.

CRA Article 14 Deadlines counts the open stages of your Article 14 reports as Breached, Overdue, Due Soon and On Track, and lists the most urgent.

ExpectedWhere
Both widgets on the layout. They read empty until Part 2 is doneOverview > Dashboards, your EU CRA layout

Part 2 — Set up each product in scope#

Repeat Steps 6 to 9 for each product. For many products, use the API (see the end of Part 2).

Step 6 — Record the product’s regulatory facts#

In the left menu, go to Explore > Assets and select the product’s Asset. The Regulatory card is near the top of the Asset page, below the Engagements, Tests, Findings and Locations tiles. Fill it in and select Submit:

FieldExampleWhat it records
CRA Product ClassImportant, Class IYour class determination. Not Applicable records that the CRA does not apply, and turns off the CRA prompts for this Asset
Support Period End2031-12-31The end of the support period
Security Contactproduct-security@example.comWhere vulnerability reports go: an email address or a URL
Disclosure Policy URLhttps://example.com/security/disclosure-policyYour coordinated vulnerability disclosure policy
NotesWho made the class determination, when, and where it is recordedThe trail an assessor will ask for
SLA LockedUnticked, unless Step 8 says otherwiseKeeps the Asset out of criticality-based SLA assignment

The empty Security Contact and Disclosure Policy URL boxes show grey example text. It is a hint, not a value: type your own.

Supports a Critical or Important Function is a DORA field; leave it unless DORA applies to you. Every field is described on Regulatory Profile.

ExpectedWhere
The values are still there after you reload the pageThe Regulatory card

Step 7 — Add the CRA regulation#

On the same Asset page, select the gear icon at the right end of the row with the Asset’s name, and choose Edit Asset. Scroll to Optional Fields and open Regulations. Type CRA in the list’s search box, tick CRA (European Union), and select Submit at the bottom of the form.

Search by the acronym: the list shows only the first few regulations until you search, and typing Cyber finds nothing.

ExpectedWhere
Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847) (CRA) is listedAsset page > Asset Overview > Regulations tab
The product is counted on the CRA rowThe Regulatory Scope widget (Step 5)

You need both Step 6 and Step 7. The Regulatory Scope widget reads the regulation; the Article 14 prompts and the assessment read the card.

Step 8 — Assign the remediation policy#

Open Edit Asset again (gear icon > Edit Asset), set SLA Configuration to the configuration from Step 4, and select Submit. Changing it recalculates the deadlines of the Asset’s existing findings; the field is locked while that runs.

Tick SLA Locked on the Regulatory card for a product whose deadlines must not be changed by a criticality-based assignment, such as one with its own support agreement.

ExpectedWhere
Service Level Agreement by Severity names your configuration and its daysThe Asset page, below Asset Overview

Step 9 — Products already on the market#

A product placed on the market before 11 December 2027 and not substantially modified since is outside most of the CRA (Article 69(2)), but Article 14 reporting still applies to it (Article 69(3)). Set it up like any other product, record the class and support period, and note the reasoning on the card. The assessment in Step 13 is where requirements that do not apply are recorded as Not Applicable, with that reasoning.

Setting up many products#

The same settings are available through the API, for a script over your product list:

An Asset has at most one profile; use PATCH /api/v2/regulatory_profiles/<id>/ to change it. See Regulatory Profile.

Part 3 — Load each release#

Repeat Steps 10 and 11 whenever you ship a release.

Step 10 — Import the release’s SBOM#

On the Asset page, select the arrow (▼) beside Locations on the tile at the top of the page and choose Import SBOM. In the dialog:

  1. SBOM Format: select CycloneDX or SPDX. There is no default, and the dialog will not submit without one.
  2. File: select Choose and pick the SBOM file.
  3. Version: the release, for example 4.1. If there is no Version field, Step 2 is not done.
  4. Leave Replace Existing Dependencies unticked unless this SBOM is the complete picture of everything the Asset carries.
  5. Select Submit.

From a build pipeline, post the same fields to the API:

ExpectedWhere
SBOM Imported with the number of dependencies processedA message at the top right
The count on the Locations tile goes upThe Asset page
The release’s componentsGET /api/v2/sbom/<asset id>/?spec=cyclonedx&version=4.1. A version never imported answers No version ‘…’ for this asset

Export SBOM and VEX, on the same Locations menu, exports the Asset as a whole; a single release is exported through the API as above.

Step 11 — Import the release’s scan results#

Import your scanners’ results as usual (gear icon > Import Scan on the Asset, a connector, or your pipeline): SCA, container, SAST and penetration tests. Set Scan Date to the day the scan ran. A finding’s deadline runs from its date, and some parsers take that date from the report file, so an old report imported without a scan date produces findings that are already past their deadline.

The CRA assessment reads these findings and tests:

  • VH-3 (regular security testing) needs a test whose target end date falls in the period and that is a static analysis test, or whose test type, title or scan type contains “scan”, “vulnerability”, “penetration” or “SAST”.
  • PI-2(a) reads open and risk-accepted findings marked Known Exploited (Step 3).
  • PI-2(b) to PI-2(m), apart from (g) and (i), read open findings whose CWE maps to the requirement.
ExpectedWhere
Findings with deadlines from your policy, counted from the scan dateThe Asset’s findings, SLA column
Findings for CVEs in the KEV catalog marked Known ExploitedAfter enrichment (Step 3)

Part 4 — When an actively exploited vulnerability is found#

Article 14 requires an early warning within 24 hours of becoming aware of an actively exploited vulnerability in your product, a notification within 72 hours, and a final report within 14 days after a corrective or mitigating measure is available. DefectDojo tracks these clocks and builds each submission package. A person decides when you are aware, and a person submits each stage on ENISA’s Single Reporting Platform.

Step 12 — Start and track an Article 14 report#

When DefectDojo prompts you. When an Asset whose CRA Product Class is not Not Applicable has an open finding marked Known Exploited, the Regulatory card shows CRA Article 14 Early Warning Check, listing each such finding with a Start CRA Report button. The finding itself shows a CRA Early Warning Check tag beside its title until a report names its CVE.

The prompt appears for any finding on the CISA KEV list, so it says a known exploited vulnerability affects the product, and that the early warning is due only if it is actively exploited in this product. It is a prompt to decide whether you are aware of active exploitation affecting this product, not a determination. DefectDojo records when the prompt first appeared, because a regulator may ask.

Start a report only for a real awareness event. A report cannot be deleted, its clocks cannot be paused, and it stays even if its Asset is deleted. Practise on a test instance.

  1. Select Start CRA Report on the finding’s row. To start one without a prompt, use Start CRA Report in the finding’s menu, or Act > CRA Article 14 Reports > New CRA Report in the left menu and pick the Asset yourself.
  2. Check Regime (Actively Exploited Vulnerability, or Severe Incident) and choose the Sensitivity.
  3. Give it a Title. Advisory Reference is filled in with the finding’s CVE when you start from a finding; separate several CVEs with commas.
  4. Awareness is recorded as now. If you became aware earlier, tick Backdate Awareness Time, enter the Awareness Time and a Reason for Backdating. The reason is kept on the report’s audit trail.
  5. Select Start Report.

The report gets a reference such as CRA-2026-00001, and the prompt shows that reference in place of the button. To open it, go to Act > CRA Article 14 Reports in the left menu, select the ⋮ menu on its row, and choose Open. The list is ordered by the nearest deadline, overdue first.

StageDueStarts
Early Warning24 hours after awarenessWhen the report is started
Notification72 hours after awarenessWhen the report is started
Final Report14 days after the corrective measure is availableWhen you select Record Corrective Measure and enter when it became available. Until then it reads Not Armed

Each stage lists what its submission must contain, and has JSON, Markdown and PDF downloads of the package. After you submit a stage on ENISA’s platform:

  1. Type the reference the platform returned in Reporting-platform reference on that stage.
  2. If you submitted earlier than now, tick Submitted Earlier Than Now and enter when.
  3. Select Record Submission.

Recording a submission adds a note to each finding on the Asset whose CVE is in Advisory Reference, and to those findings’ engagements, with the stage, time and platform reference. It does not change the findings’ status.

A check runs every 15 minutes. It sends CRA deadline due soon and CRA deadline breached notifications to the users who can view the Asset, and to superusers, through the channels each person chose for Other events (Settings > Notifications > Personal Notifications). A missed deadline is recorded and does not clear: a stage submitted after its deadline closes as breached, not met. Record each submission as soon as you make it.

A severe incident follows the same 24 and 72 hour stages, with a final report one calendar month after the notification is submitted. See CRA Article 14 Reporting.

Part 5 — Produce evidence each period#

Step 13 — Assess the product#

On the Asset page, open the Compliance tab under Asset Overview and select Compliance Profile. Open the EU Evidence tab and select Start Assessment. In the dialog:

  1. Catalog: EU CRA Vulnerability Handling.
  2. Period Start and Period End: the period you are assessing, for example a quarter.
  3. Release Assessed: the release, if the assessment is for one release.
  4. Notes: optional.
  5. Select Start Assessment, then Recompute Evidence.

Release Assessed labels the assessment and its evidence pack; the evidence is still read for the whole Asset over the period. When an Asset has several assessments, choose one in the Assessment list, or open it with Review under Regulatory Assessments on the Profile tab, which shows each period.

The assessment has 32 requirements. Once Parts 1 to 3 are done, expect:

VH-2 Remediate without delaySatisfied while no more than 5% of open findings are past their deadline and no risk acceptance has expired unhandledThe policy (Steps 4 and 8) and your remediation
VH-3 Regular security testingSatisfied when a test in the period meets the rule in Step 11Your scan imports (Step 11)
VH-4 Disclose fixed vulnerabilitiesUnknownRecorded by hand: see below

Recompute whenever the data changes, and before generating a pack. The rules behind each automated state are on CRA and DORA Evidence Packs.

Record what DefectDojo cannot measure#

Select Evidence on a requirement’s row to see its computed facts, including a note on what to record. To set its state by hand:

  1. Change Evidence State.
  2. Fill in the Override Reason that appears (Why This Requirement Does Not Apply when you choose Not Applicable). Submit stays disabled without one.
  3. Write an Assessor Narrative.
  4. Attach supporting documents with Attach a File.
  5. Select Submit.

RequirementEvidence to record
VH-4 Disclose fixed vulnerabilitiesYour published security advisories, release notes or changelog entries for the vulnerabilities fixed in the period. The computed facts list the vulnerability IDs of findings mitigated in the period, under fixed_vulnerability_ids
SP-1, SP-2 Support periodSP-1 reads the Support Period End from Step 6 and is Partially Satisfied once it is set; attach your published support policy as a file, with the justification if the period is shorter than five years. For a product covered by Article 69(2) (Step 9), set them Not Applicable with that reasoning
RP-1 to RP-6, when UnknownThat no Article 14 event occurred in the period, if that is your determination
RP-7 Inform impacted usersHow you informed users of the vulnerability and the corrective measures
PI requirementsHow each is implemented. A narrative is required before one can be set Satisfied or Partially Satisfied

A recompute never replaces a state set by hand: the Evidence view shows Manual override with the latest automated state beside it. Untick Keep Manual Override and select Submit to return to the automated state.

Step 14 — Freeze the evidence#

On the assessment, select Recompute Evidence, then Generate Evidence Pack. The pack is built in the background; reload the page after a few seconds.

ExpectedWhere
A Completed pack with OSCAL assessment results (json), marked schema validated, and EU evidence workbook (xlsx)Evidence Packs on the assessment

A pack is a frozen snapshot that later changes never alter. Generate one at the end of each period, and keep the downloads with your technical documentation. See Generate an evidence pack.

Moving to a production workflow#

  • Automate the per-release steps. Post the SBOM with its version and the scan results from the same pipeline job that builds the release, so Steps 10 and 11 never depend on someone remembering.
  • Agree the awareness decision in advance. Name who looks at a CRA Article 14 Early Warning Due prompt, how fast, and who submits to ENISA. The 24-hour clock starts at awareness, not when someone gets round to it.
  • Assess on a fixed cycle. Start each period’s assessment at the start of the period, recompute as you go, and generate the pack at the end.

What is not surfaced today#

So nobody spends time hunting for these:

  • No screen lists per-release SBOMs. They are stored per version and exported through the API.
  • The Article 14 report’s audit trail has no screen. The awareness reason is in each stage’s JSON download.
  • No dashboard widget or report reads assessment states or CRA classes. They are on the EU Evidence tab and in the evidence pack.
  • SP-2, VH-4 and RP-7 are not computed. SP-1 reads Support Period End from the Regulatory card, but the published support policy is still attached by the assessor.
  • No PDF evidence pack. The workbook and the OSCAL file are the pack.
